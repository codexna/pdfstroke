How to password protect a PDF (and remove the password)

If you’re sending documents with personal information—payslips, medical reports, a copy of your ID card—the safest option is to encrypt them with a password. PDF Stroke does it on your device with 256-bit AES encryption, the same standard Adobe Acrobat uses.

Get it free on Google Play Use it in your browser

Add a password to a PDF

  1. Open PDF Stroke and tap Protect with password. Choose the PDF.
  2. Type the password twice. Choose one that’s hard to guess: at least 8 characters, with letters and numbers.
  3. Tap Protect. An encrypted copy is saved; the original stays unchanged.
Important: if you forget the password, nobody can open the PDF, not even us. Keep it somewhere safe and send it separately from the document, through a different channel (for example, the PDF by email and the password by text message).

Remove the password from a PDF

  1. Tap Remove password and choose the protected PDF.
  2. Enter the current password.
  3. A copy without the password is saved. You can open it, edit it or merge it with other documents.

You need to know the password to remove it: the app doesn’t crack passwords on other people’s documents.

Frequently asked questions

Will it open in any PDF reader? Yes. The encrypted PDF opens in Adobe Acrobat Reader, in your browser, on your phone and in any modern PDF reader, all of which will ask for the password.

Is the password sent anywhere? No. The encryption happens on your device and the password is not stored.
